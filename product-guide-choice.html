How to choose the right Squeegees

A practical selection framework for matching squeegee material, hardness, angle, pressure, width, and edge sharpness to your printing process.

M H A D W S

Factors that affect Squeegees

Material performance and operating conditions directly influence printing stability, replacement frequency, and total process cost.

01 / PERFORMANCE

Wear Resistance

High wear resistance reduces replacement frequency during production, boosting operational efficiency and lowering overall costs.

02 / CHEMICAL COMPATIBILITY

Solvent Resistance

Modern printing inks and pastes are formulated with various chemical solvents and additives to suit different products and application environments. Squeegees lacking adequate solvent resistance are prone to swelling, deformation, and hardness reduction, directly compromising final print quality.

Technical Selection Parameters

Use the M-H-A-D-W-S framework to evaluate the main variables that determine ink transfer, print definition, blade life, and screen protection.

M

Material Selection

Select the optimal polyurethane (PU) formulation based on ink or paste chemistry to minimize squeegee deformation during printing and ensure optimal print consistency.

H

Hardness Range

Commonly ranges from 50 to 90 Shore A with a manufacturing tolerance of ±3°A.

  • 50A–65A (Soft): Provides higher ink laydown.
  • 70A–75A (Medium): Ideal for general printing applications.
  • 80A+ (Hard): Delivers sharper detail and finer line definition.
A

Angle

The standard angle ranges from 45° to 80°. Extreme angles accelerate wear on both the screen and squeegee blade. Generally, a higher printing angle results in lower ink transfer.

D

Downward Pressure / Depth

Deeper squeegee pressure exerts greater force on the printing screen, increasing wiping action and reducing ink deposit. However, deeper pressure also accelerates mesh damage, so down-pressure should generally be kept as minimal as possible.

W

Width / Free Height

Standard squeegee width ranges from 10 mm to 75 mm, with an effective free width—the exposed portion extending from the holder—typically set between 20 mm and 30 mm. This exposed width determines squeegee flexibility under pressure.

  • Shorter Extension: Higher rigidity, yielding sharper prints.
  • Longer Extension: More flexibility and extended blade life; however, excessive extension causes vibration and tip deformation, leading to moiré patterns.
S

Edge Sharpness

Edge sharpness directly dictates initial ink laydown.

  • Sharp Edge: Delivers minimal ink volume and highest image resolution.
  • Rounded / Dull Edge: Increases ink transfer, making it ideal for thick-film applications, wide lines, or full-surface flooding.
